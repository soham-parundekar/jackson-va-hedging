Why this filing rather than a later one. This is the last 10-K in which the variable annuity
guarantee sits on the balance sheet under the pre-LDTI measurement, as a carrying value that
is positive at both disclosed dates. Every filing from FY2023 onward reports a market risk
benefit that is a net asset, so a level comparison against a model that prices a single
in-force contract is meaningless. What survives the regime change is the ratio of the down
impact to the up impact, which is scale-free, and the sign of the year-on-year move. Those are
what V2 tests.

The rate shock in this filing is 50 basis points. The FY2025 filing uses 100. The shock size
is a property of the filing.

"The carrying value of variable and fixed index annuity guarantee liabilities reflects the
present value of projected benefit payments less the present value of attributed fees. These
benefit payments and fees are subject to differing degrees of discounting, as benefit payments
are generally projected to occur further in the future as compared to attributed fees. As a
result, the degree of sensitivity between the present values of projected fees as compared to
the present values of projected benefit payments may result in disproportionate sensitivity
impacts relative to the liability carrying value."

"The carrying value of our annuity guarantee liabilities reflect our contract holders' exposure
to equity market declines. When equity markets increase, this exposure and the related
liability declines."

Derived quantities used in V2, computed from the table above rather than quoted from it:
  asymmetry ratio at Dec 2021 = 1,526 / 663   = 2.30
  asymmetry ratio at Dec 2022 = 2,202 / 1,793 = 1.23
  average equity impact per 10% at Dec 2021 = (663 + 1,526) / 2   = 1,095
  average equity impact per 10% at Dec 2022 = (1,793 + 2,202) / 2 = 1,998
  carrying value fell 88% between the two dates while the average equity impact roughly doubled
